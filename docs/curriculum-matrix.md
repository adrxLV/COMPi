## Boundary and source reconciliation

- **In scope:** the week-7 lecture map explicitly includes foundations (weeks 1–4), conditionals (week 5), `for`/`range` (week 6), and list traversal/growth/change/slicing (week 7). The preparation paper also mixes all of these, including strings, `.split()`, lists and loops. [`Computation_I_Lecture_07_slides.pdf`, p. 22; `example_mini_test.pdf`, pp. 2–8, 10–11]
- **Not established as first-test material:** `while` and `break` are marked “still to come”; the course guide schedules them in week 8, nested `for` in week 9, list comprehensions in week 10, matrices in week 11, functions in weeks 12–13 and modules in week 14. Do not infer coverage merely because a later chapter of the practical book already contains those subjects. [`Computation_I_Lecture_07_slides.pdf`, p. 22; `Computation_I_Course_Guide.pdf`, p. 23; `Computation_I_Practical_Exercises_Book_2026.pdf`, PDF pp. 2–3 (contents)]
- **Nuance:** week 2 is listed on 10 or 11 September as a replacement lecture during the first calendar week, whereas practical classes begin in calendar week 2. The numbered lecture/handout sequence is the reliable learning progression here, not a claim that every numbered topic was first taught in that calendar week. [`Computation_I_Course_Guide.pdf`, pp. 9–10, 22]
- **Evidence gaps:** no separate week-7 practical handout or week-7 study PDF appeared among the supplied sources; its lecture deck is the direct week-7 source. The practical exercise book is broader than the first-test syllabus, and the preparation paper is an example rather than an official exhaustive topic list.

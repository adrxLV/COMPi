# Duolingo principles, translated for COMPi

**Research snapshot: 20 October 2026.** These are Duolingo's published descriptions, not an assertion that every course, platform or A/B variant currently behaves identically. Its 2022 home-screen account documents the rationale for a redesign, not a definitive 2026 screen specification.[2] COMPi teaches a bounded Python course for a first coding test, not a language course; apply the learning mechanisms rather than copying the brand, monetisation, mascot or exact interface. See [the UX audit](UX_AUDIT.md) for what was actually observed in COMPi.

## Principles and decisions

| Published principle | Evidence | Suitable COMPi adaptation | Boundary / test of success |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Do, then explain as needed.** Duolingo describes interactive tasks that begin with easier patterns, progress to harder applications, and offer optional hints and short explanations.[1] | Its five-part method names learning by doing, personalisation, focus, motivation and delight.[1] | Honour COMPi's [learning contract](LEARNING_CONTRACT.md): a concise concept teaching screen, then one focused question screen at a time; prompt a prediction, trace, diagnosis or code change rather than using a long passive lecture. Offer a hint without replacing the attempt. | The contract explicitly requires teaching first, so *do not* mechanically copy Duolingo's immediate-exercise opening. Compare completion, wrong-to-correct recovery and independent transfer on a later question, not clicks alone. |
| **Give the next useful step.** The 2022 guided path groups lessons into smaller units, interleaves concepts, incorporates practice and permits revisiting completed levels.[2] | The design sought to reduce uncertainty about what to do next; review is forward progress, not a separate penalty.[2] | Replace seven long week-sized instructional stops with short named concept units; show current, completed and forthcoming positions, an obvious continue action and a completion state. Put relevant prior-concept retrieval between new units where the finite exercise bank supports it, while retaining open practice. | Do not invent extra assessment items or imply a one-question unit proves mastery. Preserve prior completions, XP, attempts and drafts on migration, as the contract requires; a path redesign must not reset progress. |
| **Recover from errors before progression.** Duolingo says it reviews mistakes at lesson end and uses optional, contextual explanations; its 2026 feedback announcement describes user-controlled, timely explanations.[3][4] | Wrong responses are information for teaching, not a reason to strand a learner. | On a wrong answer, show the relevant misconception and one actionable next move: retry or revisit teaching. Require a later correct answer before advancing in the guided lesson; record the wrong attempt and recovered response separately. For code, point to failing case or a bounded hint without silently giving away a full solution before another attempt. | Distinguish *learning flow* from the open practice arena and exam simulation. Later correction should enable progression but must not retroactively grant first-try XP; do not display an invented mastery score. |
| **Space active retrieval.** Duolingo explains that a later review, especially after errors, and increasing intervals can support long-term retention; its language practice considers spacing and accuracy.[3] | Its path redesign interspersed earlier concepts and practice among new material.[2] | Revisit a previous Python concept with a different reasoning format (e.g. predict `range`, then diagnose an off-by-one loop) and surface due questions in the path as well as Mistake lab. Preserve the existing explainable review queue unless there is evidence to tune it. | Do not claim COMPi's fixed `reviewDays` or queue is Duolingo's adaptive algorithm, or that an immediate answer proves retention. Measure delayed unaided correctness across sessions. |
| **Make progress credible and optional practice accessible.** Duolingo uses short sessions, points and streaks for motivation, while its 2026 Practice-tab description provides targeted on-demand practice, including mistakes.[1][5] | A guided route and free-choice practice coexist rather than forcing all learning into one rail.[2][5] | Keep the path as the default, practice/traps/exam as deliberate alternatives, and small celebrations after a *completed* unit. Label XP as reward, first-try completion as evidence of one performance, and spaced review as an invitation. | Avoid copying hearts/energy, social competition or inflated proficiency claims. COMPi is offline-first and its supplied preparation paper has no established official score weights (`docs/DECISIONS.md`). |
| **Focus on the relevant syllabus.** Duolingo's method says standards guide content selection.[1] | Its language standards are not a Python curriculum. | Use COMPi's [Weeks 1–7 curriculum matrix](curriculum-matrix.md) to order fundamentals before synthesis and keep the exam simulator visibly original practice. Mark copied/alias or enrichment questions as such if the source does not establish them as assessment evidence. | This is a pedagogical analogy, not a claim of Duolingo endorsement or validated equivalence. |

## Design tensions and evidence limits

- **Guidance versus learner agency:** the path should suggest the next sensible step, but a locked week rail can obstruct a learner who needs targeted practice. Keep free-choice routes and backward review. Duolingo's historical path also allowed completed-level revisits.[2]
- **Explicit teaching versus discovery:** Python syntax, error categories and test-case reasoning sometimes merit an upfront worked example. The COMPi contract chooses a dedicated teaching screen; keep it short, then elicit an answer before showing the exercise explanation. Duolingo's sequence is a reference, not a mandate.[1]
- **Spacing versus limited inventory:** interleaving 52 existing exercise IDs across short units requires a content map, not duplicated questions, synthetic attempts or more XP. A single suitable question should be presented as practice, not proof of competence. Re-evaluate the plan using delayed independent answers, not claims from the Duolingo blog alone.[2][3]
- **Product claims versus local validation:** the cited blog posts report Duolingo's rationale and features, not independent evidence that identical changes improve this specific course. The audit's browser checks are Chromium desktop and emulated mobile only; there is no iOS Safari test or longitudinal learning outcome here.
